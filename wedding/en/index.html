Dress code

Dressy, but no strict rules. Imagine a festive dinner at a nice restaurant.

Women

Good to wear
  • Midi or maxi dress
  • A below-the-knee skirt with a blouse or sweater
  • Jacket, cardigan or wrap
  • Comfy heels or ankle boots
Better not
  • Trousers and trouser suits
  • White or ivory dress
  • Bare shoulders at the chuppah
  • Mini skirts, deep necklines
  • Very high stilettos

No need to cover your head: only religious married women do.

The chuppah, step by step

The ceremony is in Hebrew, and most of the evening is in Russian. If you speak English or Hebrew, someone will be next to you to translate.

  1. Step 1 of 9

    Signing the ketubah

    Witnesses sign the marriage contract, which protects the wife.

  2. Step 2 of 9

    The veil

    Alex lowers Chana's veil: he marries her soul, not just her beauty.

  3. Step 3 of 9

    Walking to the chuppah

    Our close ones walk us under the canopy, a symbol of a home open to guests.

  4. Step 4 of 9

    The circling

    Chana circles Alex seven times, like building a wall around the family.

  5. Step 5 of 9

    Wine and the first blessings

    The rabbi blesses the wine, a symbol of joy.

  6. Step 6 of 9

    The ring

    Alex gives the ring and says: “You are consecrated to me.” Now we're married.

  7. Step 7 of 9

    Reading the ketubah

    The contract is read aloud and handed to Chana.

  8. Step 8 of 9

    Seven blessings

    The rabbi and our close ones bless us. Answer “Amen”!

  9. Step 9 of 9

    Breaking the glass

    Alex breaks a glass in memory of Jerusalem, and everyone shouts “Mazal tov!” Tap it 3 times!

The chuppah is in Hebrew, the evening mostly in Russian. Someone will always be nearby to translate.

Yes to toasts, no big party games. If you'd like to say a few words, message us in advance: Ramina, our host, will find a spot for you in the programme.
